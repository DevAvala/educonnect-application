# EduConnect AI Lab: AI Career & Talent Discovery Hub

**Feature:** 4 of the current EduConnect AI Lab concepts  
**Status:** Newly proposed and added to the brainstorming plan. No implementation or AI model selected.  
**Last discussed:** 12 November 2026

## 1. Vision

Help students discover their interests, explore career possibilities, and develop talents over time. The Hub should **expand options**, not label a child or decide their future based on grades.

## 2. Core Experience

- **Interest Discovery:** Age-appropriate activities and questions about what students enjoy, value, and want to try.
- **Talent Exploration:** Give students opportunities to explore coding, writing, design, science, sports-related analysis, public speaking, entrepreneurship, and other fields.
- **Career Explorer:** Explain what different professionals do, relevant subjects, skills, example tasks, and possible education or training pathways.
- **Try-a-Career Challenges:** Small guided projects that introduce real-world work, such as designing a simple website, preparing a presentation, or investigating a science question.
- **Pathway Suggestions:** Optional subjects, skills, clubs, projects, or learning resources that connect to a student's stated interests.
- **Growth Portfolio:** A student-controlled record of completed projects, interests explored, and skills practised.

## 3. Connection to Student Brain

With appropriate permission, the Hub may use Student Brain's **topic-level academic evidence** to suggest learning opportunities or prerequisite subjects.

For example:

- A student interested in engineering who performs well in algebra may be offered a beginner engineering design challenge.
- A student interested in medicine but currently struggling with a science topic should receive a supportive learning pathway, **not** be told they cannot pursue medicine.
- A student with a passion for astronomy can explore astronomy even if it is not part of their present school syllabus.

**Important:** Exam marks alone cannot reliably determine talent, career fit, motivation, or a child's potential. Interests and project participation are additional, optional signals and should not be treated as psychological diagnoses.

## 4. Example Student Journey

1. A student selects interests such as space, mathematics, and making things.
2. The Hub introduces astronomy, physics, engineering, and related activities.
3. The student completes a guided project, for example explaining how a satellite stays in orbit.
4. The Hub suggests optional foundational lessons from the Study Companion.
5. The student saves their project to a portfolio and chooses what to explore next.

## 5. Possible Student, Parent, and School Views

**Student:** Discover careers, try activities, save projects, and explore new interests without restrictions based on class rank.

**Parent:** See the child's shared interests and completed projects, plus practical, low-cost ways to support exploration. Avoid pressure-inducing labels or rankings.

**Teacher or counsellor:** Offer guidance, review age-appropriate pathway suggestions, and recommend clubs or mentorship opportunities.

## 6. AI Responsibilities

- Explain careers in clear, age-appropriate language.
- Recommend a **range** of fields based on explicitly stated interests and optional learning evidence.
- Propose introductory projects, relevant skills, and possible next steps.
- Identify when career facts, admission requirements, or qualifications require a current verified source rather than guessing.
- Encourage student choice and human guidance rather than issuing definitive career verdicts.

## 7. Safeguards and Equity

- No deterministic statements such as “you are meant to be a doctor” or “you cannot become an engineer.”
- Never infer intelligence, personality, aptitude, or mental health diagnoses from examination marks.
- Safeguard minors' profiles, project portfolios, and any shared personal information.
- Provide broad, non-stereotyped recommendations across different backgrounds and interests.
- Include accessible, affordable pathways relevant to Nigerian schools, without excluding international opportunities.
- Separate verified career information from speculative forecasts and require a current source for time-sensitive admissions or qualification advice.
- Give students and guardians appropriate control over information shared with teachers or others.

## 8. Possible Delivery Phases (Not Approved)

1. **Explore:** Career library and interest-based discovery experiences.
2. **Try:** Guided mini-projects and optional student portfolios.
3. **Connect:** Link to Study Companion learning pathways and selected Student Brain topic insights.
4. **Guide:** Optional parent and teacher/counsellor support tools.

## 9. Open Decisions

- Which age groups and school levels should have access at launch.
- Whether portfolios are private, parent-visible, or selectively shared.
- Whether verified external career information is curated manually or sourced dynamically.
- Whether to offer mentorship, school clubs, or external opportunities later.
- Which AI models and content review policies will be used.

**Planning note:** This feature is a proposal; scope, data-sharing rules, and model choices remain open.
